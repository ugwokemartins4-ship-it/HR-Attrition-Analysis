# HR Attrition Analysis: Turning Employee Data into Actionable Business Insights with Power BI

## Project Overview

This project demonstrates an end-to-end HR analytics workflow, transforming raw employee data into an interactive Power BI dashboard designed to support workforce planning, employee performance evaluation, promotion planning, and attrition-related decision-making.

Using a dataset of **1,470 employees across three departments**, I developed a four-page analytical report that provides insights into workforce demographics, employee retrenchment, promotion eligibility, overtime distribution, performance ratings, monthly income, and other workforce characteristics.

The objective was to move beyond descriptive reporting and deliver meaningful insights that help HR professionals and organizational leaders identify workforce challenges, evaluate operational patterns, and make informed, data-driven decisions.

## Business Problem

Organizations need reliable workforce data to understand employee distribution, identify potential workforce risks, evaluate career progression opportunities, and allocate resources effectively.

However, raw employee data often makes it difficult to identify patterns and communicate findings to decision-makers.

This project addresses that challenge by transforming employee records into an interactive reporting solution that enables stakeholders to explore workforce metrics, identify areas requiring attention, and develop targeted HR strategies.

## Project Objectives

The analysis focused on answering the following business questions:

* What is the organization's workforce composition by gender, department, job role, job level, and marital status?
* How many employees are flagged for retrenchment, and which job roles have the highest concentration?
* How many employees are due for promotion, and what does this indicate about career progression?
* How widespread is overtime, and what additional workforce factors should HR investigate?
* How are employee performance ratings distributed?
* How do monthly income and commute distance vary across employee groups?
* How can HR use these findings to improve workforce planning, employee development, and retention strategies?

## Tools and Technologies

**1. Power BI:** Developed the interactive dashboard, KPI cards, charts, tables, slicers, and report navigation.

**2. Power Query:** Imported, cleaned, transformed, and prepared the employee dataset for analysis.

**3. DAX (Data Analysis Expressions):** Created measures to calculate workforce KPIs, employee counts, gender distribution, retrenchment figures, promotion eligibility, and monthly income metrics.

**4. Microsoft PowerPoint:** Designed the dashboard wireframe to establish the report structure, visual hierarchy, layout, and navigation before implementation.

**5. Canva:** Designed the dashboard homepage to create a professional and visually engaging landing page.

## Project Workflow

### 1. Data Cleaning and Transformation

Using Power Query, I prepared the raw dataset for analysis by:

* Reviewing column data types and data consistency.
* Checking for duplicate records and missing or inconsistent values.
* Standardizing relevant text fields.
* Renaming columns for improved readability.
* Creating categories for commute distance to support segmented analysis.
* Preparing the dataset for calculations and visualization in Power BI.

### 2. Data Modeling and DAX Calculations

I developed reusable DAX measures to calculate key HR metrics and support interactive analysis.

Examples include:

* Total employees.
* Male and female employee counts.
* Gender distribution percentages.
* Employees flagged for retrenchment.
* Retrenchment percentage.
* Employees due for promotion.
* Total monthly income.
* Average monthly income per employee.

These calculations provided a consistent foundation for the dashboard's KPIs and visualizations.

### 3. Dashboard Wireframing and Design

Before developing the report, I created a wireframe in PowerPoint to plan the layout and establish a consistent visual structure.

I used Canva to design the homepage and Power BI to build the interactive analytical report.

The design process focused on readability, visual consistency, intuitive navigation, and presenting relevant information in a format suitable for business decision-makers.

## Key Findings

The analysis produced several important workforce observations:

**1. Workforce Demographics**

The organization has 1,470 employees, comprising 882 males (60%) and 588 females (40%). This distribution provides a baseline for evaluating workforce representation and reviewing equal-opportunity initiatives.

**2. Retrenchment Analysis**

A total of 117 employees (7.96%) are flagged for retrenchment, while 1,353 (92.04%) are classified as remaining in service. The job-role breakdown highlights managers as a group requiring further review, with 44 of 102 managers flagged.

**3. Promotion Opportunities**

Only 72 employees (4.90%) are identified as due for promotion, while 1,398 (95.10%) are not. This finding supports further investigation into promotion criteria, employee eligibility, and career development opportunities.

**4. Departmental Workforce Distribution**

Research & Development accounts for 961 employees, followed by Sales with 446 and Human Resources with 63. These figures provide a basis for reviewing departmental staffing requirements and resource allocation.

**5. Overtime and Performance Ratings**

A total of 416 employees (28.3%) work overtime, while 1,054 (71.7%) do not. Additionally, 226 employees (15.37%) are classified as highly rated, compared with 1,244 (84.63%) classified as low-rated under the project's rating classification.

These patterns highlight areas for further investigation into workload distribution, performance evaluation criteria, employee support, and workforce engagement.

*Note: These findings describe the dataset and its classifications. They do not independently establish the causes of retrenchment, low ratings, or employee attrition.*

## Business Recommendations

Based on the findings, I developed the following recommendations:

* **Workforce diversity:** Review recruitment, promotion, and career development practices to support fair access to opportunities and improve gender representation where appropriate.
* **Retrenchment review:** Validate the criteria used to flag employees for retrenchment and evaluate performance, skills, business requirements, and potential redeployment options before final decisions.
* **Career progression:** Review promotion eligibility requirements and strengthen mentoring, training, and professional development programs.
* **Resource optimization:** Assess departmental staffing levels against operational requirements, particularly within Research & Development and Sales.
* **Employee performance:** Investigate the factors contributing to the current performance-rating distribution and introduce targeted coaching and development initiatives.
* **Workload and wellbeing:** Examine overtime patterns alongside job satisfaction, working conditions, and workload distribution to identify opportunities for improvement.

These recommendations are intended to support evidence-based decisions rather than assume that the observed patterns establish causation.
